# We Cannot Continue Like This

Our societies face immense challenges: climate change, economic insecurity, international conflict, and technological disruption. Meeting them requires responsible decisions made with a long-term view. At the same time, polarisation, mistrust, and hostility make constructive public debate increasingly difficult.

**The left against the right. The right against the left.** Too often, opponents are treated as enemies rather than people who belong to the same society. Yet we will still have to live together tomorrow. People we disagree with may raise legitimate concerns. We need to take their ideas seriously, examine their arguments, and find common ground—without tolerating hatred, discrimination, or attacks on fundamental rights.

Social media can intensify outrage, disinformation, and radicalisation. But the problem is not confined to any platform or one political camp. Had we responded sooner to credible warnings and societal grievances, some developments might have been avoided. We cannot reverse everything that has happened.

**That is why we must act now. Continuing as before is not an acceptable answer.**

What if, instead of arguing endlessly about **who** governs, we reconsidered **how** government itself should work?

What if governing were a temporary civic responsibility rather than a professional career? What if expertise, independently verifiable selection by lot, informed participation, and effective checks on power formed the basis of public decision-making?

This question has led to a first proposal: the **Draft Constitution of a Knowledge-Based Sortition Democracy**.

**This is not a constitution handed down to the public.** It is developed openly, using open-source principles: a possible new form of government **shaped by the people themselves**. Everyone can read it, challenge it, propose amendments, and try to disprove its assumptions.

We are not seeking a new ideology or victory for one political camp. We are testing whether a governance system can be built that withstands abuse of power and serious criticism—including criticism of its own foundations. Human dignity, fundamental rights, and peaceful public debate are non-negotiable.

**Our invitation is not: “Agree with us.” It is: “Find the flaws. Bring better ideas. Help fix them.”**

**By people. For people. Open to debate. Improved together.**
