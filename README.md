# OpenCivicLab

**An open, non-partisan project to develop and critically examine alternative ways of governing.**

> This is a **discussion draft**, not an enacted constitution or a call to seize power. Human rights, the rule of law, checks and balances, and the freedom to criticise this project are essential tests of every proposal.

## Why this project exists

Societies face challenges that require competent, responsible, long-term decisions. Yet public debate is increasingly affected by polarisation, disinformation, radicalising dynamics, and the incentives of attention-driven social media. These problems have many causes; social media are not the only one.

**The left against the right. The right against the left.** This antagonism makes progress harder. But we must still live together. Legitimate concerns and evidence-based arguments deserve attention regardless of which democratic political camp raises them. Had certain problems been addressed earlier, some conflicts might have been less severe. We cannot undo the past; we can begin to act constructively now.

Rather than asking only **who should govern**, we ask a more fundamental question: **Can public institutions be designed to resist the abuse of power, poor decisions, and manipulation more effectively?**

The answer should not be imposed by one person or political group. It should be developed **in public, through evidence, criticism, and revision**.

## Core ideas under discussion

- **Temporary civic service** in place of a permanent political career.
- **Qualifications and sortition** (selection by lot) for executive leadership, alongside a Chamber of Expertise and a representative Citizens' Chamber.
- **Informed referendums** with universal access to education and topic-specific knowledge assessment.
- **Independent adversarial review** of major public decisions, explicitly tasked with finding flaws and counter-evidence.
- **Independent integrity checks** and regular reviews of those in office.
- A **two-year advisory transition** after changes of government.
- **Strictly purpose-bound, temporary emergency powers** that expire when they are no longer necessary.
- In the future, an independently validated **AI review** of changes to eligibility requirements, with a qualified and legally overridable veto—but only if such technology becomes demonstrably reliable enough.

## This is not a finished constitutional model

We especially welcome **strong objections**. Could suitability screening produce an elite? Can psychological assessments be fair, scientifically sound, and compatible with human rights? Would knowledge-gated voting violate political equality? Could AI become another tool for capturing power?

We ask contributors to submit **concrete failure scenarios**, verifiable evidence, and actionable alternatives—not merely agreement. No person, institution, or assumption is beyond criticism.

## Participate

Open an *Issue* to report a weakness or propose an improvement. Submit a *Pull Request* with a proposed replacement text. In public contributions, distinguish verifiable facts, uncertainty, normative judgements, and hypotheses.
